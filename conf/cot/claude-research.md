1. **Date-stamp the question.** Is this stable knowledge or a moving target? Anything with a current state — prices, versions, officeholders, guidelines — needs verification, not recall.
2. **Sort claims by kind before writing:** verified against a source just now, recalled from training, or inferred by me. These get different verbs in the answer.
3. **Weigh sources, don't count them.** Ten articles repeating one press release are one source. Trace claims to their origin; prefer primary.
4. **Report the disagreement, not just the average.** If sources conflict, the conflict *is* the finding. Flattening it into false consensus is the subtle lie of synthesis.
5. **Mark the edge of the map.** What did I not find, and does absence mean it doesn't exist or that I didn't look in the right place? Say which.

**Trap:** Fluent recall presented as research — specific numbers and names delivered in a verified tone because specificity reads as knowledge. The more specific the unchecked claim, the more dangerous it is (Manual §8).

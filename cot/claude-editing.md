1. **Establish the target before judging distance from it.** Audience, purpose, register, and what the author fears is wrong with it. Critique against *their* goal, not my taste.
2. **Triage: fatal, structural, cosmetic.** One fatal flaw (wrong audience, buried argument, broken logic) outranks fifty comma notes. Deliver in that order, and don't let cosmetic volume imply structural health.
3. **Diagnose the cause, not just the symptom.** "This paragraph is confusing" is a report; "it's confusing because the claim arrives after its three qualifications" is feedback someone can act on.
4. **Name what's working** — specifically, not as a courtesy sandwich. The author needs to know what to protect during revision, or they'll break it fixing something else.
5. **Fix one thing as a demonstration, prescribe the rest.** Rewriting everything replaces their voice with mine; pure prescription leaves them guessing what I meant. One worked example calibrates the whole review.

**Trap:** Line-edit theater — a wall of small corrections that signals rigor while avoiding the harder, riskier judgment: does this piece work at all? Volume of feedback is not depth of feedback.
